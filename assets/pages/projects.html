The Panel Kiln

Description

The innovation is a set of essentially flat (but could be corrugated or shaped for strength) wall segments called panels that can be transported flat and then relatively easily positioned to create a six-sided structure (earth bottom, optional open top, two ends, and two sides of one or more segments) to be a pyrolysis kiln.

Panel kilns can operate either with Flame Cap (FC) pyrolysis (with no intentional air entering the lower half of the created cavity) or with some adjustments as Top-Lit UpDraft (TLUD) pyrolysis (with intentional air entering at the bottom of the cavity)

How It Works

Entry of the biomass feedstock is from the top, as delivered by any method for handling biomass.

Feedstock shape will impact the delivery: long straight stalks, reeds, bamboo, etc. can be dropped along the full length of the kiln.

Short chunky can be dispersed by “sprinkling” or spreading. Small biomass such as pits of fruit and probably corn cobs might be delivered by gravity from elevated hoppers; etc Residues (maize stalks, husks, sugarcane trash, woody shrubs) are layered and burned under controlled low-oxygen conditions.

Incorporates sequential and moderated addition of dry biomass in layers with pyrolytic radiant heat from above and accumulation of created biochar in the lower cavity where minimal air is to be entering.Smoke is consumed by the flame curtain, producing biochar instead of ash

Benefits

  • Operation in batch or cumulative batch mode (addition of biomass input during operations) as Flame Cap or TLUD pyrolysis
  • Produce larger quantities of biochar with larger devices
  • Have faster operations for loading the biomass and unloading the biochar
  • Accept biomass types and shapes/sizes not currently served
  • Offer mobility and/or portability of the kilns
  • Maintain or improve the quality of emissions and efficiencies
  • Have significantly lower capital and operational costs per unit of biochar production

ROCC Kiln

Description

A RoCC kiln, short for Rotatable Covered Cavity kiln, is a type of pyrolysis kiln used for biochar production and thermal energy generation. It features a covered cavity with an open top design where pyrolysis of biomass occurs under a cap of flames.

The kiln is rotatable on demand, allowing mixing of biomass inside to ensure complete pyrolysis and to facilitate easy emptying of char. Its covered design protects the flame from wind and rain, retains heat longer, and allows directed use of created heat via chimneys.

The rotatable feature also helps automate and scale up production by mechanically mixing the biomass and char inside the kiln. RoCC kilns are available in various sizes and are patented technology, with applications in biochar production, carbon sequestration, and providing thermal energy while using biomass efficiently.

How It Works

The RoCC kiln works by using a rotatable, covered cavity design to facilitate pyrolysis of biomass into charcoal or biochar. The kiln is a fire-resistant container that is mostly enclosed except for a portal through which air, fuel, and emissions flow.

It is supported on wheels or an axle that allows it to be rotated along its longitudinal axis when desired.Rotation serves several key purposes: it mixes and tumbles the biomass and partially pyrolyzed material inside, exposing it evenly to heat and breaking apart charcoal clumps.

This improves the completeness and efficiency of the pyrolysis process. The rotation also facilitates positioning the portal for fuel feeding, controlling air intake, directing emissions and heat, and allowing discharge of char when pyrolysis is complete.

The covered design protects the flame from wind and rain, retains heat longer inside, and enables directing the generated heat for additional uses via chimneys or hoods.

The kiln operation involves different positions for loading biomass, normal operation with fuel feeding, mixing and tumbling to expose not pyrolyzed feedstock, and char removal. These functions allow larger volume and more controlled biochar production compared to open top flame-cap kilns.The process is initiated by loading biomass and a small amount of charcoal to ensure ignition. Once the fire is stable, the kiln is slowly rotated to the operating position to maintain an even fire.

As pyrolysis progresses, the kiln is rotated back and forth to mix contents and enhance pyrolysis coverage. Char can be removed through the portal without fully stopping the process, aided by internal prongs that retain biomass not pyrolyzed while allowing char to exit, This operation improves efficiency, safety, and scalability of pyrolysis processes using biomass.

RoCC Kilns and Biochar in Kenya

Introduction to the RoCC Biochar Kiln

Benefits

  • Protected flame:The covered cavity design mostly shields the flame from wind and rain, which improves flame stability and combustion efficiency
  • Directed heat use: The created heat can be channeled for other uses via chimneys
  • Rotatable for mixing:The ability to rotate the kiln mixes biomass and char inside to ensure complete pyrolysis and exposure to heat, overcoming limitations of traditional static kilns
  • Easier char removal:Rotation facilitates emptying the biochar without exposing operators to excessive heat
  • Worker safety:Partial shielding from radiant heat and reduced risk of accidental fires compared to fully open flame kilns.
  • Longer heat retention:The covered design retains heat longer within the kiln, enhancing pyrolysis effectiveness
  • Potential for mechanization and scale-up:The rotation can be automated for larger production scales
  • Possibly cleaner emissions:Better combustion due to controlled airflows and heat retention may reduce emissions
  • Good biochar quality:Produces biochar typically above 550°C, favorable for quality char production

Climate Role

Renewable Energy and Fossil Fuel Displacement

The heat generated from the pyrolysis process can be directed for use in heating or hot water, replacing fossil fuel energy sources, thus contributing to lower overall fossil fuel emissions

Climate Smart Agriculture

The biochar enhances soil health, increases crop yields, and improves water retention, supporting climate resilience in agricultural systems and further mitigating climate change impacts

Reduced Emissions

The covered and mostly enclosed design improves combustion efficiency, leading to cleaner emissions with potential reductions in methane and other greenhouse gases compared to traditional open biomass burning. The kiln's heat retention and air flow control aids in more complete pyrolysis and cleaner gas combustion

Carbon Sequestration

The biochar produced by the RoCC kiln represents stable carbon that can be stored long-term in soil, effectively removing CO2 from the atmosphere. For example, one 1-barrel RoCC kiln produces about 50 kg of biochar in 10 hours, which corresponds to roughly 125 kg CO2-equivalent in long-term carbon removal and storage. This biochar can generate revenue in carbon markets, boosting climate-smart practices

Clean Cookstoves

Millions of rural households in Kenya still rely on smoky open fires or charcoal, leading to deforestation and health risks. To reduce deforestation, improve household air quality and promote climate action on the community level we introduced clean cookstoves in 2023

We began with PowerPellet TLUD-ND stoves which run on pellets to address cooking energy needs while producing biochar and co-generating electricity. Pelletjiko stoves came in later to address households that were connected to the electricity grid

Activities

  • Deployed 300 cookstoves to date in partnership with the Rotary Club of Bungoma Magharibi and the Sunset Rotary Club of Bloomington-Normal
  • Worked with 10 Women Self-help groups ( WSGs) to own, operate and manage cookstoves and distribute pellets
  • Provided training on cookstove use, management, biochar collection and safety

Project Target

  • 800 stoves by 2025, unlocking ~$7,500/year from carbon credits for sustainability
  • Circular economy solution for clean cooking, energy access and biochar production

Results

  • They are more fuel-efficient, leading to substantial savings in fuel costs, time reduction on firewood collection and creates more time for more income-generating activities or leisure
  • The significant reduction in particulate emissions lowers the risk of respiratory diseases, benefits mostly women and children who spend more time near the stove
  • The use of pellets efficiently reduces firewood consumption, helping conserve forests, reduce emissions of greenhouse gases and pollutants, contributing to climate change mitigation and environmental preservation
  • Adoption of clean cookstoves empowers women by reducing their labor burden in cooking and fuel collection
  • Women’s groups earn 10% margin on pellet distribution thereby creating jobs and income opportunities for their operations and merry go round activities
  • On average each stove produces ~100 kg biochar/year = 220 kg CO₂e removed annually which is charged and applied to their kitchen gardens for soil sequestration

TLUD Cookstove

Description

Modular, portable flame-cap kilns made of steel panels. Designed for medium-scale farmer groups.

Technology

  • Runs on pelletized sugarcane bagasse from ACE Pellets
  • Produces biochar rich in fixed carbon (≈62%)
  • Equipped with a ThermoElectric Generator (TEG) producing 8W of power for phone charging and LED lights

How It Works

Household stoves that provide clean cooking while generating biochar as a byproduct

Benefits

  • Reduces indoor air pollution
  • Provides electricity in advanced models (e.g., PowerPellet TLUD with 8W ThermoElectric Generator)
  • Farmers collect 5–10 kg of biochar per week for soils

Climate Role

Each stove = 0.5–1 tCO₂e removed annually
